Separation science

Two ways to take a mixture apart

One sorts particles by a property they do not share. The other converts the particle you want to remove into something else. The difference is whether anything leaves the process as a different substance than it entered.

01 · Physical separation

Exploit a difference in property

The components are already distinct substances sitting side by side. Sorting them needs only a property they do not share: size, density, boiling point, charge, solubility, magnetism.

Mixture of two substances

filter · distil · centrifuge

no bonds touched

The same two substances, now apart

Every particle leaves the process as the particle that entered it. Only its location changed, which is why remixing puts you back where you started.

02 · Chemical separation

Convert what you want to remove

Here the target shares whatever property you would sort on, or is not a separate substance at all: it is bound inside a compound. A reagent reacts with it selectively, and the product of that reaction is what gets taken away.

What leaves the process is not what entered it. Getting the original substance back takes a second reaction, not a second mixing.

Where the line blurs

The boundary is a matter of degree rather than a hard rule. Dissolving is counted as physical, though solvation involves real interaction between solute and solvent. Chromatography separates by repeated, reversible binding: chemical interaction doing physical work. Solvent extraction sits on either side depending on whether the target is complexed on the way across.

The working test stays the same. Ask what you would find if you analysed the output: the substances you started with, or different ones.
