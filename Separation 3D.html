Two routes to take CO₂ out of a gas stream

The same target molecule, removed two ways. One route sorts molecules by a property they do not share and never touches a bond. The other converts the molecule into something else, and what leaves the vessel is not what entered it. Drag to orbit either one.

Route 01 · Physical

Sort on a property they do not share

CO₂ and N₂ are already separate substances drifting side by side. Chill the stream and they part company on boiling point alone — CO₂ condenses out while nitrogen stays a gas. Not one bond is broken, so warming the two back together simply remakes the mixture.

Route 02 · Chemical

Convert what you want to remove

Slaked lime reacts with CO₂ selectively. A hydroxide proton moves across, calcium keeps one oxygen, and the carbon picks it up as carbonate — solid CaCO₃ drops out and water is left behind. The carbon leaves the vessel as a different substance, so no amount of mixing brings the CO₂ back.

  • ExploitsReactivity
  • ReactionCO₂ + Ca(OH)₂
  • ProductsCaCO₃ + H₂O
  • Bonds altered2 broken, 2 formed
  • To undoCalcine at ~900 °C
Geometries are drawn to real shapes — CO₂ linear, water bent at 104.5°, carbonate trigonal planar — at ball-and-stick scale rather than true van der Waals radii, so bonds stay visible. Carbonate is shown as one Lewis structure; its three C–O bonds are in reality equivalent and delocalised.
